What does a P/E ratio tell you — and what doesn't it?

The price-to-earnings ratio is a common valuation measure. It is useful, but it cannot tell the whole story on its own.

What is P/E?

The price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio compares a company's share price with its earnings per share (EPS). In simple terms, it describes how much investors are paying for each unit of the company's reported earnings.

P/E = Share price ÷ Earnings per share (EPS)

A simple illustration

If a share trades at ₹200 and its relevant EPS is ₹10, its P/E ratio is 20. This is a mathematical illustration, not a valuation opinion or a recommendation.

Why can P/E be useful?

It can help compare valuation levels between companies in the same industry, or compare a company's current valuation with its own historical range. Comparisons are more meaningful when accounting periods and earnings definitions are consistent.

What P/E cannot tell you

  • It does not independently measure business quality.
  • It may be misleading when earnings are unusually high or low.
  • It is not useful in the usual way when earnings are negative.
  • Different industries naturally have different economics and valuation ranges.
  • It does not show debt, cash flow, governance or future growth by itself.

Use it with other information

Consider revenue and profit growth, return ratios, debt, cash flow, management quality, competitive position and the risks facing the business. Read the notes to financial statements and compare companies carefully.

A low P/E does not automatically mean a share is cheap, and a high P/E does not automatically mean it is expensive. Context matters.
