BUSINESS • BEGINNER GUIDE

How does a company actually make money?

A company's popularity is not the same as its profitability. To understand a business, start by following the money.

1. Revenue: money coming in

Revenue is the income a business earns from selling products or providing services before subtracting its expenses. For a retailer, it may come from product sales. For a bank, it may include interest income and fees. For a software company, it may include subscriptions and licensing.

2. Costs: money going out

Businesses spend money to operate. Costs can include raw materials, employee salaries, rent, logistics, marketing, technology and interest on borrowings. Some costs rise with sales; others may remain relatively fixed over a period.

3. Profit: what remains

Profit is what remains after relevant expenses are deducted. Different profit measures answer different questions. Operating profit focuses on the business's operations, while net profit also reflects items such as interest, taxes and other non-operating effects.

4. Cash flow matters too

Accounting profit and cash generated are not always the same. A company may record a sale but collect the money later. That is why investors also study cash flow, working capital and the quality of earnings.

Questions to ask

  • What does the company sell, and who pays for it?
  • Are sales recurring or one-time?
  • Which costs are growing fastest?
  • Does reported profit translate into operating cash flow?
  • Does the company need significant borrowing or capital expenditure to grow?

The useful habit: understand the business model first, then examine its financial statements. A familiar brand is not automatically a good investment.

Educational article. For company-specific analysis, verify figures against audited annual reports and official exchange disclosures.
